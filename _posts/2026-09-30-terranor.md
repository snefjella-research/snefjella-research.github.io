## Summary

Terranor is growing faster than its own targets, and adjusted EBITA margins are moving up from a low base. The market already accepts the growth story. What it does not yet accept is that the margin plan will turn into cash.

- **Growth is not the problem.** Sales grew 23% in each of the first two quarters of 2026, and the order backlog reached SEK 7.3bn at the end of June.
- **The 2026 margin guide is demanding.** Hitting the guided 3.9% adjusted EBITA margin for 2026 requires roughly 5.3% in the second half, against 3.4% in the same period of 2025.
- **Cash conversion is better than it looks, but not yet proven.** Reported free cash flow after leases was negative in 2024 and SEK 55m in 2025. Adjusted for one-off costs and working capital swings, underlying conversion is around 50–75% of adjusted EBITA.
- **What would make us wrong:** Weak Q3 and Q4 margins, or a new round of restructuring costs, would push the margin story out by another year.

Our base case values the shares at around **SEK 48**, about 25% above the current price, with a wide range between bear and bull outcomes.

## How Terranor makes money

Terranor operates and maintains roads for public clients in Sweden, Finland and Denmark. In winter that means snow clearing and gritting. In summer it means repairs, asphalt and drainage. The company also maintains green spaces, installs road safety equipment and runs light infrastructure projects.

Most revenue comes from contracts won in public tenders, typically running four to six years with options to extend. A contract is priced once, at the tender, and then delivers revenue over its life. That makes the order backlog a good guide to future sales, but it also means that a contract won at a poor price stays in the numbers for years.

Margins are made in three places: the price at the tender, how densely contracts sit in one area (so that crews and machines can be shared), and extra work outside the base contract, which is priced separately and closer to cost-plus. Extra work peaks in winter, which makes the fourth quarter the most profitable of the year.

The business was carved out of NCC. Mutares bought the Swedish and Finnish operations in 2020 and the Danish business in 2021, listed the company on Nasdaq First North in June 2025, and sold its last shares in May 2026. Terranor had 682 employees at the end of 2025.

**Sweden** is the growth engine and the only market that makes a meaningful profit today. Sales grew 37% in Q1 2026.

**Finland** is flat by design. Management is walking away from low-margin work, and claims related to older state contracts are still being negotiated. Margins are close to zero.

**Denmark** is mostly winter work, so the first quarter carries costs before the higher-margin months. New state contracts are running to plan, and large municipal contracts in Ikast-Brande and Copenhagen were added in Q2 2026.

## The market

Funding is the strongest part of the case. Sweden's national transport plan raises spending on road operation and maintenance by 48% for 2026–2037. In the 2026 Swedish tender season, Terranor won 31% of the contract volume awarded.

The customers are national road agencies and municipalities. Credit risk is close to zero, and budgets are set years ahead. The main competitors are the state-owned Svevia in Sweden and Destia in Finland, plus the road divisions of the large construction groups. Svevia's operating margin was 2.2% in 2025, which shows how thin margins in this industry can be.

## The core question: earnings vs. cash

Adjusted EBITA has grown, but cash flow has moved around much more. The bridge below starts from reported operating cash flow and deducts what a shareholder never sees: capital spending and lease payments.

| SEK m | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Adjusted EBITA | 89.5 | 98.1 |
| Items affecting comparability | -13.0 | -72.4 |
| Reported operating cash flow | 110.0 | 213.9 |
| of which change in working capital | -49.1 | +56.0 |
| Capex (property, plant and equipment) | -27.5 | -17.4 |
| Lease repayments | -92.2 | -127.0 |
| Lease interest | -10.6 | -14.1 |
| **Free cash flow after leases** | **-20.3** | **55.4** |
| FCF excluding working capital swing | 28.8 | -0.6 |
| FCF excluding working capital and one-offs | 41.8 | 71.8 |
| **Underlying cash conversion (vs. adj. EBITA)** | **47%** | **73%** |

Free cash flow figures are our calculation. We assume lease interest is reported in financing cash flow and that items affecting comparability were paid in cash in the same year.

Three things stand out.

**1. Leases are the largest cash cost below EBITDA.** Lease payments rose from SEK 103m to SEK 141m in 2025, and lease liabilities from SEK 295m to SEK 402m. Machines and vehicles are leased contract by contract, so every new contract adds lease debt. This is a real cost, and it is why EBITDA-based multiples flatter the company.

**2. The 2025 cash flow was helped by working capital.** Strip out the SEK 56m working capital inflow and 2025 free cash flow was roughly zero. The improvement in 2025 was mostly timing.

**3. The one-offs are shrinking.** Items affecting comparability were SEK 72m in 2025, mainly listing costs and restructuring in Sweden and Finland. In H1 2026 they fell to SEK 6m, against SEK 36m a year earlier. If that holds, the gap between adjusted EBITA and cash narrows quickly.

The conclusion is that Terranor's adjusted figures are not misleading, but they describe the business the company is trying to become. The cash flow statement still describes the business it was. That gap is the reason the stock trades at a discount, and it closes only if margins rise without new one-off costs.

## Can the 2026 guide be met?

Guidance implies SEK 156m of adjusted EBITA in 2026. After SEK 47m in H1, the second half needs about SEK 109m. On second-half sales of roughly SEK 2.05bn, that is a margin of about 5.3%. In the second half of 2025 the margin was 3.4%.

The fourth quarter is seasonally strong, and several new Swedish contracts start in September, so a step-up is expected. But a 1.9 percentage point jump is large. The Q3 report on 10 November will show whether the company is on track. Missing the 2026 guide would not break the case, but it would make the 2028 target of 5% harder to believe.

## Risks

- **Re-tendering:** Contracts come up for tender again. Losing large Swedish contracts would hit group profit hardest, since Sweden makes almost all of it.
- **Finland and Denmark:** The margin plan depends on old, low-margin contracts rolling off. The Finnish claims are still unresolved.
- **Input costs:** Fuel, salt and asphalt prices. Most contracts are indexed, but with a delay.
- **Weather:** A mild winter means less extra work in Q4 and Q1, which are the quarters that carry the margin.
- **Earnings quality:** If restructuring costs return, adjusted EBITA will keep overstating the cash the business produces.

## Catalysts

- **Q3 2026 report (10 November 2026):** the first test of the second-half margin step-up.
- **Q4 2026 report:** whether the 2026 guide is met.
- **2027 Swedish tender season:** whether market share holds at disciplined prices.
- **Dividend:** a payout of at least 50% of net income would make the cash story visible to investors.

## Conclusion

Terranor is a well-funded, low-risk business with a margin problem it is visibly fixing. The growth and the backlog are not in doubt. The stock will rerate when margins and cash flow move together for a few quarters in a row. Until then, the discount is earned.
