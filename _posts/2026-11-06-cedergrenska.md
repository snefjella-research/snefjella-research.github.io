## Summary

Cedergrenska buys small Swedish independent schools, runs them more efficiently and keeps buying. Sales grew 30% in 2025/26 at a stable 7.6% EBITA margin, and management guides for at least 10% growth and at least SEK 7.30 in earnings per share in 2026/27. At SEK 47.40 the shares trade at about 6.5x guided earnings.

The discount is political. The 13 September 2026 election gave the opposition bloc 176 seats against 173 for the Tidö parties, and two candidates for prime minister have since been voted down. The Social Democrats have campaigned on ending profits in schools. Until Sweden has a government and a clear school policy, the stock is unlikely to rerate.

- **Thesis point 1:** The acquisition model works: buy small operators cheaply, cut central costs, grow earnings faster than sales.
- **Thesis point 2:** The political worst case is less likely than the price implies, because the result was close and any government will be weak.
- **What would make us wrong:** A government with the votes to restrict profits broadly, not only at poorly run schools.

Our base case values the shares at **SEK 55**.

## How Cedergrenska makes money

Cedergrenska runs preschools, compulsory schools, upper-secondary schools (gymnasier) and adult education in Sweden, plus its own school-meal and cleaning companies. Revenue is the municipal school voucher (skolpeng) paid per pupil, so the company cannot set prices. Profit comes from filling places and running schools efficiently.

The growth model is acquisitions. Since 2011 the group has bought around 30 schools, mostly from single-school owners, who make up about 80% of independent school operators. Each acquisition moves onto shared HR, IT, finance, purchasing and teacher training. The 2024 purchase of Aprendere (18 units) is the clearest example: bought at about 8x EBITDA, with management expecting synergies to cut that to under 5x EBITA by 2026/27.

In July 2026 the group added Skärgårdsgymnasiet in Åkersberga (over 500 pupils, about SEK 85m in sales) and Carl Wahren gymnasium (about SEK 20m), together with a property company.

## What changed since the spring

Three things matter for anyone who read the bull case written earlier this year:

1. **Q4 margin dipped.** The April–June quarter came in at a 6.7% EBITA margin, down from 9.0% a year earlier, even though full-year margins held. <!-- FILL IN: reason given in the year-end report. -->
2. **The election did not settle anything.** School stocks rose about 4% the day after the vote because the market read the narrow result as making a profit ban hard to pass. But no government has been formed. Magdalena Andersson (S) was voted down in October and Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist (C) on 3 November. Ulf Kristersson (M) is now exploring a government. If four votes fail, Sweden holds a new election.
3. **Listing and accounting change.** Cedergrenska plans to move to IFRS and to Nasdaq Stockholm's main market. Under IFRS goodwill is no longer amortised, which lifts reported profit, but school leases come onto the balance sheet as debt. ## Risks

- **Profit restrictions:** a broad ban or cap on dividends from schools would undermine the model.
- **Proposed rules already on the table:** a five-year profit distribution ban for new and acquired schools, loss of quality grants for operators that pay dividends, and new disclosure duties.
- **Integration:** a bad acquisition would hurt both earnings and the market's trust in the model.
- **Voucher levels:** municipal school vouchers that lag cost inflation squeeze margins.

## Catalysts

- Formation of a government, or a new election.
- The first-quarter 2026/27 report.
- Move to IFRS and the main market, which widens the investor base.
- Further acquisitions at single-digit EBITA multiples.

## Conclusion

The company is doing its job: growing, integrating acquisitions and paying a dividend. The share price is waiting on Swedish politics, and that question is now more open than it was before the election. At 6.5x earnings, investors are paid to wait, but they need a view on politics more than on the business.
