## Summary

*Published 15 October 2026. Updated 7 November 2026 with the CEO's share purchases, the expiry of the warrants and the share price on 26 October. The financial figures are from the quarter ended 1 August 2026; the next report is due in December.*

GameStop is two things in one listing. One is a shrinking video game retailer that has turned itself into a collectibles business and now earns real operating profit. The other is a very large pile of financial assets: about USD 5.1bn in cash and securities, a USD 4.9bn stake in eBay and a small bitcoin position, against USD 2.8bn of zero-coupon convertible notes.

- **The shops are more profitable than they were.** In the second quarter (ended 1 August 2026) sales fell 19%, but operating income rose from USD 66m to USD 160m as the gross margin went from 29% to 44%.
- **The mix has changed.** Collectibles are now 45% of sales and grew 57% in the quarter, while video games fell 47% and pre-owned and refurbished products fell 32%.
- **Net income is not operating income.** Of the USD 299m net income in the quarter, USD 238m came from non-operating gains on the eBay position. Interest income of USD 77m is also not retail profit.
- **The discount has partly reopened.** The shares peaked at USD 28.02 on 15 October and had fallen to USD 23.90 by 26 October. At that price we estimate the market pays about USD 4.9bn for the operating business, or roughly 8x annualised first-half operating income.
- **The CEO is buying.** Ryan Cohen bought about 4.1 million shares for roughly USD 97m in five open-market purchases between 10 September and 13 October, and now owns about 42.5 million shares directly.
- **The warrants are gone.** The USD 32 warrants expired worthless on 30 October with the shares well below the exercise price, which removes that source of dilution.
- **What would make us wrong:** a successful eBay deal on good terms, or a stronger collectibles business than we assume, would justify a higher value. A weak holiday season or a loss on the eBay position would do the opposite.

Our base case values the shares at around **USD 26**, about 9% above the price on 26 October. The range runs from USD 19 to USD 34.

The fiscal year ends around 1 February, so the second quarter ended on 1 August 2026. GameStop now reports collectibles, video games and pre-owned and refurbished products instead of hardware, software and collectibles, and has recast the earlier periods. Margins and growth rates are our calculation.

## What the business looks like now

GameStop sells video games, consoles, trading cards and other collectibles through its stores and online. The old model depended on new game releases and on trading in used games. Digital downloads have eaten most of that for years, and the numbers still show it: video game sales almost halved in the quarter.

Collectibles are the part that has worked. Trading cards, figures and similar items carry higher margins than consoles and boxed games, and they pull people into stores. Collectibles sales were USD 705m in the first half, up from USD 439m a year earlier, and they were the largest category in the second quarter.

Part of the profit jump comes from cost cuts. SG&A fell by USD 32m in the quarter and by USD 58m over the half, helped by store closures. The rest comes from the mix: a quarter in which low-margin hardware shrinks and high-margin collectibles grow will lift the gross margin by itself.

The open question is how much of this is repeatable. Collectibles can be a fashion, and a hot trading card market does not last forever. Sales rose about 14% in the first quarter but fell 19% in the second, so the profit growth depends on the margin holding while the top line is uneven.

## Earnings quality

Three points matter before anyone puts a multiple on the reported numbers.

**Net income is mostly financial.** In the first half, operating income was USD 303m out of USD 688m net income. Net interest income added USD 161m. The remainder is mainly gains on the eBay position, where GameStop's derivative holding was converted into shares, and on the shares themselves. These gains move with eBay's share price and will not repeat on a schedule.

**Interest income depends on cash and rates.** USD 77m a quarter is a high return on about USD 5bn, and it falls when rates fall or when cash is spent. The exchange of convertible notes in September took out USD 358m of cash. We value the cash at face value and treat operating income as the profit of the shops alone, so interest is not counted twice.

**Diluted EPS is lower than basic.** Diluted EPS in the quarter was USD 0.51, against USD 0.67 basic, because the convertible notes would add shares at current prices. The notes are 0% and carry no cash interest, but they are not free: they convert into shares if the stock is high enough.

## The balance sheet and the eBay position

|---|---|

| Digital assets and related receivables (bitcoin) | 294 |
| Convertible notes due 2030, principal | 1,500 |
| Convertible notes due 2032, principal | 2,700 |

**The convertible notes.** At the end of August GameStop agreed to exchange about USD 1.4bn of the 2030 and 2032 notes for about 55.5 million new shares and USD 358m in cash. The exchange closed around 3 September. About USD 2.8bn of notes remain: roughly USD 1.1bn due 2030 and USD 1.7bn due 2032. The share count rose from 449 million to 504.5 million (3 September). We use the higher number and treat the remaining notes as debt at face value, which is cautious. If the share price rises enough that the notes convert, the debt turns into shares instead.

**eBay.** GameStop made an offer of USD 125 per share for eBay in May, which eBay's board rejected as neither credible nor attractive. GameStop has since built a stake of just under 10%. At the 1 August price the stake was worth USD 4.9bn. We use that figure in the valuation rather than a later price, so a later eBay price would shift our numbers. Each USD 10 move in eBay is worth about USD 434m, or USD 0.86 per GameStop share.

**Bitcoin.** GameStop reports about 4,700 bitcoin, carried as a receivable of USD 294m. It is small compared with the other assets, but it adds volatility, and the company booked a loss on digital assets in the quarter.

## Valuation

At USD 23.90 per share (close on 26 October 2026, the latest we verified) and 504.5 million shares, the market value is about USD 12.1bn. The financial assets less the notes, using the numbers above, come to about USD 7.1bn:

| USD m | |
|---|---|
| Cash and securities, after the USD 358m paid in the exchange | 4,702 |
| eBay stake | 4,947 |
| Bitcoin | 294 |
| Convertible notes at face value | -2,800 |
| **Net financial assets** | **7,143** |

Taking those away from the market value leaves about USD 4.9bn for the operating business. Against first-half operating income of USD 304m, annualised to USD 607m, that is around 8x EBIT. At the peak on 15 October (USD 28.02) the same calculation gave about USD 7.0bn and 11.5x.

We value the operating business on a normalised annual operating income, because the first half may flatter the full year and because sales are shrinking. The eBay stake is taken at market value in the base case, with a haircut in the bear case and a small premium in the bull case.

| Scenario | Operating income | EV/EBIT | Operating value | eBay stake | Net cash and bitcoin | Value per share (USD) | vs. today |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | USD 400m | 8x | USD 3.2bn | USD 4.0bn | USD 2.2bn | 19 | -21% |
| Base | USD 550m | 11x | USD 6.05bn | USD 4.9bn | USD 2.2bn | 26 | +9% |
| Bull | USD 700m | 14x | USD 9.8bn | USD 5.4bn | USD 2.2bn | 34 | +42% |

"Net cash and bitcoin" is cash and securities plus bitcoin, less the notes at face value. The bear case assumes the collectibles margin fades and sales fall further. The bull case assumes the margin holds and sales stabilise. One turn of EV/EBIT is worth about USD 550m, or USD 1.1 per share.

The result is more balanced than it looked at USD 28. At USD 23.90 the market pays about 8x EBIT for the shops and counts the financial assets at the values above, which leaves a modest margin of safety in the base case. The downside to USD 19 is real if the collectibles margin fades, so the case needs the eBay stake and the cash to be worth what the balance sheet says. We have not updated the eBay stake to a later share price, so that remains the largest open input.

## Insider buying

Ryan Cohen, the CEO and chairman, has been buying shares in the open market.

After the last purchase Cohen owned about 42.5 million shares directly, or roughly 8.4% of the 504.5 million outstanding. A Schedule 13D amendment put his beneficial stake at about 9.1% when about 3.7 million warrants were included. Those warrants have since expired, so the direct holding is the number that counts.

The purchases are worth noting but not over-reading. He bought at prices from USD 20 to USD 27 and kept buying as the price rose, which signals confidence in the strategy. It does not say anything about the eBay outcome, and he holds the shares as the person who decides what the company does with its capital. Another insider sold shares worth about USD 0.4m in the same period, a much smaller amount.

**Pay and alignment.** What sets Cohen apart from most chief executives is how he is paid. GameStop's 2026 proxy statement says he takes no salary, no cash bonus and no time-vested stock. His only compensation is a performance-based option award that pays nothing unless the market value of the company more than doubles to a sustained USD 20bn and cumulative earnings targets are met. He has also added about USD 97m of his own money since September. Compare that with eBay, where, according to a summary of its 2026 proxy statement, chief executive Jamie Iannone received about USD 28m in 2025. An owner who is paid only if shareholders do well, and who buys with his own cash, is a real advantage and deserves some weight in the valuation. We have not added a premium for it. The same award also means shareholders would be diluted if the targets are reached, and one person's control over capital allocation cuts both ways, as the eBay pursuit shows.

## Risks

- **Capital allocation:** the largest risk is what management does with USD 10bn of financial assets. The eBay approach has already cost USD 358m in cash for the note exchange and tied up USD 4.9bn in one share.
- **eBay's share price:** the stake is a single large listed position. A fall in eBay hits GameStop's earnings directly, as the gains in the second quarter show.
- **Uneven sales:** revenue fell 19% in the second quarter after rising in the first. If collectibles stop growing, the profit growth stops with it.
- **Collectibles cyclicality:** card and figure markets can turn quickly.
- **Dilution:** USD 2.8bn of convertible notes could add shares if the stock rises. The USD 32 warrants expired worthless on 30 October, so they are no longer a source of dilution.
- **Interest rates:** lower rates cut the USD 77m a quarter of interest income.
- **Bitcoin:** a small but volatile holding, with a recent loss.
- **Key person:** Ryan Cohen drives the strategy, including the eBay pursuit.

## Catalysts

- **Q3 results (expected in December):** whether the margin holds at around 40% without the second quarter's cost cuts.
- **The holiday season:** the quarter with the most collectibles demand.
- **More insider buying or selling,** reported on Form 4.
- **Any move on eBay,** whether a renewed offer, a board seat or a sale of the stake.
- **Further note exchanges or repurchases,** which would change the share count and the debt.

## Conclusion

GameStop is a well-capitalised company that has found a more profitable way to run its shops, while holding financial assets worth roughly 60% of its market value at the October low. At USD 23.90 the shares trade somewhat below what we think the pieces are worth, with a CEO who has put about USD 97m of his own money in since September and no warrant overhang. The margin of safety is moderate, not large. The case rests on what happens to the eBay stake and on whether collectibles can carry the retail business, so we would want to see the December report, and the margin through the holiday quarter, before treating it as a core position.
